Some People Find Beauty in Art Galleries; I Find It in Plant Resilience

October 7, 2025

← All posts
October 7, 2025 · exRNAlab

There is a special kind of elegance in how plants face the world. They cannot move away from danger, yet they constantly adapt. They adjust their metabolism, fine-tune their gene expression, and even communicate with their neighbors through signals we are only beginning to understand.

As a molecular geneticist, I have spent years looking into the invisible conversations that help plants survive. These exchanges happen at the molecular level, through RNA molecules that act as messengers, connecting cells, tissues, and even different organisms. Plants use these messages to respond to stress, build alliances with microbes, and reprogram themselves to thrive in changing environments. The more I learn, the more convinced I am that resilience is not simply a trait that plants possess.

It is a dynamic art form, shaped by evolution and expressed through molecular creativity.

When I think about the experiments we run in the lab, I often find beauty not only in the data but in the process itself. Watching a seed germinate, a root branch in search of nutrients, or a leaf recover after drought reminds me that resilience can be quiet, slow, and profoundly effective. To me, studying plants is like studying a living masterpiece that rewrites itself with every drought, every pathogen, or every sunrise. Every season, plants edit their own story of persistence and adaptation.

There is something deeply human about this. Plants teach us that strength can coexist with flexibility, that growth sometimes means letting go, and that cooperation is as vital as competition. These are lessons I carry with me not just in science, but in life as a mother, a mentor, and a scientist navigating complex systems, both biological and social.

If you are also fascinated by what we can learn from the natural world, I highly recommend Lessons from Plants by Beronda Montgomery. It is a thoughtful and inspiring exploration of how plant life offers insights into growth, adaptation, and community. Like a good experiment, it invites us to observe carefully and think deeply about what it means to thrive together.
